Elementa’s **22 August 2026 Dichotomy beta feedback** sets out the next areas of development: combat, narrative, open-world exploration and performance. It is a response to that test, with proposed improvements and future features. It is not a list of changes verified in the launch build.

This guide separates what the notice describes from what would need a later implementation announcement or gameplay check. It covers systems and development plans without revealing a case solution.

## How does this notice fit the other announcements?

| Official material | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| July combat and deduction previews | Descriptions of the mechanics introduced for that test, including switching during charged actions and branching deductions. |
| 22 August beta feedback | Problems identified during the test and areas the team intended to improve next. |
| 31 October launch announcement | The announced release date and platforms. It does not individually confirm completion of every August proposal. |

Keep both a source’s date and its purpose in view. A feature appearing in an optimization plan is different from an instruction you can follow in a current game menu. The [launch brief](../launch-preparation/) and [platform guide](../platforms-downloads/) handle release questions separately.

## Dialogue History: can you reread conversations?

The English notice says **Dialogue History** will be added in a future update so players can revisit conversations and case details. The Japanese heading is **バックログ機能**. These are the corresponding terms in the two official versions of this announcement.

The intended benefit is clear: reviewing what someone said can help when returning to an investigation. The notice does not give a release date, a menu path or a rule about how much dialogue will be retained. It also does not say this history replaces the Evidence Board or unlocks an ending automatically.

For the announced board and restart behavior, read [Curious Deductions](../curious-deductions/). Until you can check the available in-game interface, your [personal notes](../../exploration/) can keep observations separate from theories; this is our note-taking suggestion.

## Combat: clearer feedback and guidance

The feedback identifies camera control and target visibility as concerns in encounters involving groups, flying enemies and fast-moving enemies. The planned work includes enemy placement and camera rules. Animations, effects, sounds and enemy reactions are also listed as areas for further polish, with more distinct feedback for different characters and weapons.

A separate section addresses information that was hard to notice: support buffs, **Reactors**, **Catalysts** and some status effects. Elementa says it intends to adjust both the combat interface and tutorial guidance. **Catalysts / 触媒** is a confirmed term here, but the notice does not explain its complete equipment rules or identify it as another name for Motive.

These are plans to improve how combat is communicated. They do not supply new parry windows, skill multipliers or proof that a particular character’s damage has changed. Our [combat reference](../combat-basics/) keeps the July mechanics and this later feedback in their respective contexts.

## Story presentation and the Revealer Light

The notice proposes further work on main-story visuals, stage flow, sound and animation. It also discusses controls and camera behavior in sequences such as stealth and assassination. Those references describe areas being refined, rather than a complete walkthrough of those sequences.

Elementa also plans more frequent use of detective mechanics such as the **Revealer Light / 痕跡ライト**. This establishes its official localized name and its place in the discussion of detective gameplay. It does not explain an activation button, detection range, resource cost or which specific clues it reveals.

For deduction quests, the announcement describes **Curious Deductions** as having multiple branching endings and says more similar content is planned. It gives no total quest count or complete list of conclusions.

## Exploration: interiors, NPCs and the Silverium Pegasus

For the city, the proposed improvements include more accessible interiors, stronger area identity, lighting and environmental detail. NPC appearance and behavior are also areas of focus, with the aim of better expressing occupations and personalities.

The movement section names the **Silverium Pegasus / シルバーリキッド・ペガサス**, with further improvements planned for riding and flying controls. It also proposes more collectible resources and interactive objects, alongside clearer map information, exploration guidance and camera control.

The notice supplies neither an unlock quest for the Pegasus nor flight limits, mount costs, collectible totals or a map route. The [Morgue Street guide](../morgue-street/) provides a concrete district introduction; it remains a setting reference while reproducible travel instructions await gameplay records.

## Performance plans and mobile support

The August notice acknowledges performance and compatibility work and refers to introducing a mobile version in future tests. Read that wording at its original date. It is not a current device-requirement table or a measured performance result.

The later October launch announcement lists PC, PlayStation 5, iOS and Android. That platform announcement answers a different question from whether a particular optimization was completed or how the game runs on your device. Check [official platform links](../platforms-downloads/) for current requirements and availability.
