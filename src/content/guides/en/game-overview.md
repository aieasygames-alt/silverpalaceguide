**Silver Palace is an open-world detective action RPG from Elementa.** Its official introduction combines investigations, a Victorian-inspired city and combat with real-time character switching. You play a detective returning to Silvernia, working with companions while tracing the city’s secrets.

This overview explains how those parts fit together in the announcements. It covers the opening premise and introductory world background, without case answers or later story outcomes. The [beginner guide](../beginner-guide/) provides a practical reading order when you are ready to prepare.

## The setting: why Silvernia matters

Silvernia is a city transformed by **Silverium**, the resource at the center of its industrial growth. Elementa’s launch announcement describes rival factions competing over it, with conspiracies beneath the city’s prosperity. The contrast between modern industry and hidden conflicts gives investigations a wider setting than an isolated crime scene.

The Detective’s official biography adds a personal reason for returning: after three years away, the protagonist comes back to investigate the death of a loved one. It also describes a fragile justice system and opaque noble politics. Those are opening premises, rather than a solution to the mystery.

Two Detective identities are displayed on the official character pages with a shared biography. Our [character archive](../../characters/#detective) groups those introductions together; the shared text does not establish differences in skills or progression.

## Investigations: clues, deductions and different conclusions

The launch announcement describes investigating cases and following clues with companions. A more specific July preview introduces **Curious Deductions**, a mode with branching endings and an **Evidence Board**.

That preview says **Restart Deduction** can return the board’s progress after an ending so you can pursue another conclusion. This makes the distinction between the evidence you observed and the explanation you chose useful to keep in mind. The source does not establish that every quest follows the same structure or that restarting a deduction resets unrelated account progress.

The [deduction mechanics reference](../curious-deductions/) explains those terms. If you want to preserve the mystery, read the [hint-level guide](../reading-case-hints/) before opening a future case walkthrough.

## Exploration: a city with distinct districts

Elementa describes free exploration across city districts and hidden corners. Its July **Morgue Street** introduction offers one concrete example: an area within **High Peers**, with landmarks and background about the people and threats found there.

This gives the setting recognizable places to investigate and revisit. It does not yet supply a reproducible route, collectible total or travel-time estimate. The [Morgue Street reference](../morgue-street/) keeps that development-period district description together, while your [field notebook](../../exploration/) can hold landmarks and questions from your own play.

## Combat: switching characters while actions continue

The announced combat is real-time, with character switching and coordinated actions. The July combat article makes that concrete through **Synchronised Combat**: switching during a Charged Attack or Charged Skill allows the outgoing character to finish the action before leaving.

The same article describes a yellow attack cue for **Parries**, **Executions** against Stunned enemies, and **Resonance** built through attacks. These are separate mechanisms to recognize, rather than a guaranteed chain where every parry produces an execution. Resonance effects are described as linked to a character’s **Reactor** attribute.

Read the [combat guide](../combat-basics/) for the individual interactions and their test-era scope. Character-specific equipment descriptions help illustrate the cast, but cannot establish a complete launch skill kit or an optimal rotation.

## The cast: people with roles in the city

The official introductions connect characters to everyday institutions and professions: police work, bartending, medicine and more. Their occupations also provide context for the equipment described in some profiles. An occupation alone does not define a gameplay class.

**Lorin**, for example, is introduced as a Chief Inspector seeking the Detective’s help with the difficulties facing a new police organization. His electric glove is deliberately restrained to avoid lethal injuries. The [Lorin dossier](../../characters/lorin/) explores that background and the corresponding Japanese name, **ローレン・警視長**.

## Where to go next

The announcements present investigation, exploration and combat as parts of the same detective adventure.
